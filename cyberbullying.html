Cyberbullying

Learn what cyberbullying is and how to respond to it safely.

What is Cyberbullying?

Cyberbullying is when someone uses the internet, social media, messaging apps, or other digital platforms to hurt, threaten, embarrass, or target another person.

How does it happen?

  • Sending hurtful or threatening messages.
  • Sharing someone's private photos or information without permission.
  • Posting embarrassing comments or rumours about someone.
  • Leaving someone out of online groups to hurt them.

How to stay safe?

  • Don't respond to hurtful messages if doing so could make things worse.
  • Save evidence, such as screenshots.
  • Block and report the person on the platform.
  • Tell a trusted adult if it continues or makes you feel unsafe.
  • Be kind to others online and respect their privacy.

Forms of Cyberbullying

  • Rumours:Spreading embarrassing or false information about someone online.
  • Impersonation: Pretending to be someone else online to harm their reputation.
  • Exclusion: Deliberately leaving someone out of online groups to hurt them.
  • Outing: Sharing someone's private information or secrets without permission.

Where Can You Get Help?

If you experience cyberbullying, remember that you do not have to deal with it alone.

  • Talk to a trusted adult, parent, or teacher.
  • Use the platform's block and report features.
  • Keep screenshots of harmful messages as evidence.
  • If you feel unsafe, ask a trusted adult for help immediately.
